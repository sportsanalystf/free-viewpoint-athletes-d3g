D · Direct the replay · steps 9–10

Find the players and the key moment

WHAT IT DOES

From the rig: up direction, dome centre and radius. Players are dense clusters of athlete Gaussians. Key moment: the ball’s highest point, or else peak player speed.

WHY

A replay is about one moment. Stray “floaters” once pushed the camera into a wall, so they must go.

WHAT WE GOT

Basketball: freeze at frame 59, the ball’s peak; 662 floaters removed. Synthetic tests: within 1 frame.

Left: ball height and player speed over time. Right: kept players vs removed floaters

The director starts with steps nine and ten. From the camera rig alone it works out which way is up, where the centre of the dome is and how big it is. Players are the athlete Gaussians that sit in dense clusters; loose “floaters” are removed, because in an early version they pushed the camera into a wall. Then it picks the key moment: the ball’s highest point if it can see a ball, otherwise the moment of peak speed. For basketball it removed 662 floaters and froze at frame 59, the ball’s peak; you can see the orange ball-height curve peak there. On synthetic tests it lands within one frame.
