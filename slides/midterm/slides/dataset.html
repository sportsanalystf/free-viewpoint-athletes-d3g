Our data is PanopticSports. It comes from the CMU Panoptic Studio, a dome lined with cameras, and was released together with Dynamic 3D Gaussians. There are six short clips, 150 frames each, of one or two people doing a sports movement: passing a basketball, stacking boxes, throwing a football, juggling, swinging a softball bat and swinging a tennis racket. Each frame is seen by 31 synchronized HD cameras. 27 are for training and 4 are held out so we can grade the result. Every image comes with its calibration and a foreground mask, and the raw capture also has 3D skeletons, which I aligned to the clips.
